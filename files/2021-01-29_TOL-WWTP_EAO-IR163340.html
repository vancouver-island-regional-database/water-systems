Re: Non-compliance Advisory Letter, Permit 120, 245 A Oyster Cove Road, Ladysmith BC, Effluent
On January 25, 2021, Ministry of Environment and Climate Change Strategy, Environmental Protection Division staff
conducted an inspection of your facility, Town of Ladysmith located at 245 A Oyster Cove Road, Ladysmith BC with
authorization number 120 under the Environmental Management Act.
This Advisory, the alleged violations and the circumstances to which it refers will form part of the compliance history of
Town of Ladysmith, and will be taken into account in the event of future non-compliance.
Please note that this authorization is considered to be out of compliance until such a time as it can be confirmed to meet
the authorization requirements.
Inspection Details:
On January 25, 2021, Ministry of Environment and Climate Change Strategy (Ministry) Environmental Protection Officer
Darren Stewart (Ministry Staff) conducted a planned office-based inspection of the Town of Ladysmith (Permittee)
Municipal Sewerage System (Facility) located in Ladysmith, British Columbia to verify compliance with Permit 120 (Permit).
The Permit authorizes the discharge of effluent from a municipal sewage system to Ladysmith Harbour. The Permit was
first issued on March 12, 1965, and most recently amended on January 10, 1994.
The Town of Ladysmith is currently undergoing the process of updating their discharge authorization to an Operational
Certificate issued under their approved Stage 3, Liquid Waste Management Plan

The period assessed within this inspection report is from January 1, 2020, to December 31, 2020 (inspection period) and
included a review of the following information:
• Quarterly (Q) water quality data reports (WQ DATA) submitted by the town of Ladsmith to the Ministry during the
inspection period (2020 Q1 - Q4) which includes daily flow rate records and effluent water quality data.
• 2020 Yearly Bypass Report for the Ladysmith Wastewater Treatment Plant submitted January 09, 2021.
• any non-compliance reports (NCRs) and Dangerous Goods Incident Reports (DGIRs) reported to the Ministry in 2020, i.e.
"trigger events".

The scope of this inspection was focused on monitoring data review, completed for reporting purposes related to the annual
Conditional Management Plan (CMP) which is to provide enhanced management of the molluscan shellfish harvest in
waters adjacent to the Town of Ladysmith wastewater treatment facility.
Below are the authorization clauses that were assessed for compliance during this inspection, as well as the associated
details/findings and any actions required.

Requirement Description:

2. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS, 2.1 Maintenance of Works and Emergency
Procedures
2.1: The Permittee shall inspect the pollution control works regularly and maintain them in
good working order. In the event of an emergency or condition beyond the control of the
Permittee which prevents continuing operation of the approved method of pollution control,
the Permittee shall immediately notify the Regional Waste Manager and take appropriate
remedial action.

Details/Findings:

On June 23, 2020 (DGIR 201029) the Town of Ladysmith reported a series of failures on
their Salsnes primary filters, and were forced to run on only one out of the two filters after
which the filter mesh on the only operable Salsnes filter ripped, causing a catastrophic
failure. The report also specified that after this second filter failure, the plant flow could no
longer reach secondary treatment and effluent was flowing into the spirogestor (after
primary treatment). The spirogestor began flowing to the chlorine contact tank, where it
received chlorination and dechlorination prior to discharge through the outfall pipe.
The Town of Ladysmith immediately notified the Ministry via the Emergency Management
BC (EMBC) hotline and initiated the appropriate remedial action.

Requirement Description:

2. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS, 2.2 Bypasses
2.2: The discharge of effluent which has bypassed the designated treatment works is
prohibited unless the consent of the Regional Waste Manager is obtained and confirmed in
writing.

Details/Findings:

The 2020 Summary of Bypasses at the Wastewater Treatment Plant report submitted by
the Town of Ladysmith to the Ministry on January 09, 2021, detailed two bypasses that
occurred during the inspection period.
On January 31, 2020 (DGIR 193885) a significant rain event led to 638 m3 of effluent
bypassing the designated treatment works.
On June 23, 2020 (DGIR 201029) a series of failures on the Salsnes primary filters led to
6899 m3 of effluent bypassing the designated treatment works.
This discharge of effluent which bypassed the designated treatment works did not receive
the consent of the Regional Waste Manager (now Director).

Requirement Description:

2. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS, 2.12 Operator Certification
2.12: If the facility is classified by the BCWWOCPS (the Program) at Level II or higher, the
Permittee shall ensure that all operators of the facility shall be certified by the Program to a
Class I level, at a minimum, by December 1, 1994.
Operators in Training: The Permittee shall ensure that all operators in training (OIT)
working at the facility classified by the BCWWOCPS at Level II or higher shall be required
to successfully pass an OIT examination within three (3) months of commencement of
employment at the facility. The OIT certificate shall be valid for fifteen (15) months from the
date of issue. Prior to the expiry date of the OIT certificate, but not sooner than twelve (12)
months from the date when the OIT commenced facility operation, the OIT shall
successfully complete a Class I certification examination in order to continue to operate at
the facility.
Chief Operator: Level II or Higher: If the facility is classified by the BCWWOCPS at level II
or higher, the Permittee shall designate at least one operator to be the "Chief Operator" of
the facility by December 1, 1996. The "Chief Operator" shall be certified at a Class II level,
at a minimum. After December 1, 1996, no person shall have "Direct Responsible Charge",
as defined by the BCWWOCPS, of a municipal wastewater treatment facility classified at
Level II or higher unless they possess a valid operator's certificate not more than one level
below the classification level of the facility.
Chief Operator Level III and IV: If the facility is classified by the BCWWOCPS at level III,
the Permittee shall designate a "Chief Operator", certified at a Class III level by December
1, 1998. If the facility is classified by the BCWWOCPS at Level IV, the Permittee shall
designate a "Chief Operator" certified at a Class IV level by December 1, 1998.

Details/Findings:

Michael Brown is listed as the Chief Operator and certified to an appropriate level by the
EOCP.

3. MONITORING AND REPORTING REQUIREMENTS, 3.1 Discharge Monitoring, 3.1.1
Sampling and Analysis
3.1.1: The Permittee shall install a suitable sampling facility and obtain a composite sample
of the effluent from the treatment plant effluent once every month. The sample is to consist
of four grab samples taken over a two-hour period at maximum flow and mixed to form a

and transporting the sample to adequately control temperature and avoid contamination,
breakage, etc.
Obtain analyses of the sample for the following:
5-day Biochemical Oxygen Demand;
Total Suspended Solids.

Details/Findings:

Following a review of the 2020 WQ Data, it was confirmed that sampling and analyses
were performed for Biochemical Oxygen Demand and Total Suspended Solids as required.
Fecal coliforms samples were also collected and analyzed on a monthly basis.
