                        INTRODUCTION


      Ladysmith Harbour, situated on the east coast of Vancouver
Island, is an enclosed bay opening in a southeast direction into
Stuart Channel.

      Historically , Ladysmith Harbour has been one of the most
productive shellfish areas on the east coast of Vancouver Island.
Closures to the direct marketi ng of oysters found within Lady­
smith Harbour, however, have been imposed since 1964 due to
bacterial contamination.

      Three sawmills are si tuated on the harbour shore, two near
the Townshi p of Ladysmith.    The harbour provides protection for
both intertidal and subtidal log storage.

      Chum (Oncorhynchus keta) and coho (0. kisutch) salmon, as
well as steelhead (Salmo gairdneri) , and cutthroat .(§_. clarki
clarki) trout, utilize streams flowing i nto the harbour.    Prior
to 1964, areas within the harbour were utilized as herring spawn
sites.   Subsequent reduction of herring spawn, and concern over
further alienation of salmonid and herring rearing areas resulted
in a survey of the fisheries resource by the Habitat Protection
Unit, with the following objectives:

(1)   to i dentify sensitive stream,' estuary, and herring spawn
      sites within Ladysmith Harbour.
(2)   to determine the impact of existing log handling and
      storage on (1) .

                           PROCEDURES
       Ladysmith Harbour was visited by the writer from November
6, 1974 to November 8, 1974.     The purpose of this trip was to
survey various aspects of the fisheries (both salmon and herring)
resource present in the area, and to establish the extent of
harbour area presently utilized by log handling operations.

       In order to survey one aspect of utilization of the harbour
by rearing salmonids, it was necessary to assess visually the
extent of vegetation associated with the three stream outlets
(as well as peripheral areas throughout the harbour), and to
take visual substrate evaluations at various sample sites.

       Spawning records were exami ned to .obtain a 10 y ear average
of escapement for the following streams, all of which find out­
let along the southern side of Ladysmith Harbour:
1.     Bush Creek
2.     Holland Creek
3.     Rocky Creek, and
4.     Stocking Lake Creek.

       Herring spawn records for the past twenty-three years were
examined in order to ascertain the historical utilization of
the i nterior reaches of the harbour as spawning grounds.     These
results were compared with more recent herring spawn distri­
bution.

                           SUMMARY


1.   Sensitive fisheries zones identified within Ladysmith
     Harbour include stream estuaries, herring spawn si tes,
     salt marshes and shellfish areas.
2.   Annual salmon escapements (10 year mean) to the three
     major producing streams (Bush Creek, Holland Creek,
     Stocking Lake Creek) ' total 11, 880 chum and 260 coho.
3.   Herring spawn distribution (Figure 1) has decreased
     �arkedly since 1964 within the harbour limits. Sites
     presently utilized include Dunsmuir Island, Sharpe Point
     and Coffin Point.
4.   Vegetation below the suprati dal fringe is restricted
     generally to fucus E..2£.· and salt marsh communities (Appendix
     A) . Salt marsh communities are associated with the three
     major salmon-producing streams and the foreshore of Ivy
     Green Provincial Park.
5.   Intertidal log storage where coincidental with salt marsh
     communities appears to accelerate erosion of these sites.
6.   Extensi ve log booming and storage occurs within Ladysmith
     Harbour. Water based log sorting and A-frame dump facili­
     ties are present on both sides of the harbour.
7.   Existing log handling operations coincidental with identi ­
     fied sensitive fisheries zones include:
     (a)   intertidal log storage adjacent to Ivy Green Park
     (b)   Rocky Creek Estuary

                       RECOMMENDATIONS


     To maintain a viable fisheries resource within Ladysmith
Harbour, i t is recommended that:
1.   Log storage and/or dumping facilities not be permitted
     within, or immediately adjacent to, those areas delineated
     as fisheries sensitive zones (Figure 1) .
2.   Existing log storage be phased out adjacent to the following
     zones:
     (a)   Salt marsh adjacent to Ivy Green Park
     (b)   Bush Creek lower intertidal estuarine zone
     If deemed necessary by Fisheries Personnel, further reme­
dial �easures be implemented on completion of the above.
3.   Should further studies indicate the possibility of restoring
     historically utilized herring spawn sites within Ladysmith
     Harbour and the salmon escapement to Rocky Creek, existing
     industrial activities at i dentified sites, be phased out
     and remedial measures implemented"
4.   Further studies be implemented to determine:
     (a)   potential for salmon enhancement to Bush, Holland and
           Stocking Lake Creeks.
     (b)   distribution and food utilization of rearing salmonids
           within Ladysmith Harbour.
5-   Early discussions be undertaken between representatives of
     the appropriate Industries and resource agencies to develop
     specific protective procedures and formalize phase-out
     sequences.

Salt Marsh definition

     The uppermost region of the estuary, immediately below
the supratidal area, has been described as a salt marsh.   The
following are some typical characteristics of a salt marsh: 3


1.   generally bordering bays and estuaries,
2.   inundated only by the highest tides,
     typically rather flat, except for shallow pools and
     narrow channels,
4.   characterized by the presence of a flowering plant,
     Salicornia virginica,
     a prevalent form of true grass, salt grass
