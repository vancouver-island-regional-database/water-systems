Details/Findings:

On January 28, 2019, Ministry of Environment and Climate Change Strategy (Ministry)
Officer Darren Stewart (Officer Stewart) conducted an office review inspection of the Town
of Ladysmith wastewater treatment facility located in Ladysmith, BC to verify compliance
with Permit PE-120. Permit 120 authorizes the discharge of effluent to Ladysmith Harbour
from a municipal sewage system.
The Permit was first issued on March 12, 1965, and last amended on January 10, 2994.
The inspection period for this report is from January 01, 2018 to December 31, 2018 and
included a review of the following documents:
- 2018 Quarterly Water Quality Data Reports (2018 Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 WQ DATA)
- 2018 outfall inspection report submitted to the Ministry on September 13, 2018
- Trigger Event Report for December 20, 2018 event, submitted to the Ministry on
December 27, 2018
- 2018 yearly bypass report submitted to the Ministry on January 14, 2019
The following 13 exceedances of the maximum rate at which effluent may be discharged
through the sewage treatment plant were noted on the following dates as reported in the
quarterly WQ DATA:
January 18, 2018 = 6904 m3
January 21, 2018 = 7966 m3
January 22, 2018 = 6179 m3
January 27, 2018 = 6951 m3
January 28, 2018 = 9093 m3
January 29, 2018 = 11475 m3
January 30, 2018 = 6191 m3
December 13, 2018 = 6361 m3
December 16, 2018 = 6117 m3
December 18, 2018 = 6918 m3
December 20, 2018 = 7747 m3
December 22, 2018 = 6617 m3
December 23, 2018 = 7444 m3
There was one daily report of flows discharged to the bypass outfall, which occurred on

Details/Findings:

On December 20, 2018, as reported by Town of Ladysmith, there was an emergency
condition (power surges and outage) caused by a condition beyond the control of the
Permittee (wind storm) that prevented continuing operation of the approved method of
pollution control.
The Permittee immediately notified Emergency Management BC by calling the Spill
Reporting Hotline (DGIR# 183468) and took appropriate remedial action as outlined in the
Trigger Event Report provided to the Ministry on December 27, 2018.
The report described a bypass of 153 m3 of screened sewage (heavily diluted by rain
water) through our overflow bypass, as well as 568 m3 that did not receive secondary
treatment - flowing through screening and the spirogester without receipt of disinfection.
An on site electrician, remote assistance from an electrical engineer and on site
technicians stopped the bypass after approximately 2.5 hours.

Compliance:

In

Requirement Description:

2. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS, 2.2 Bypasses
2.2: The discharge of effluent which has bypassed the designated treatment works is
prohibited unless the consent of the Regional Waste Manager is obtained and confirmed in
writing.

Details/Findings:

On February 21, 2018 (DGIR#174028) the town of Ladysmith completed a dye-test
inspection of their wastewater treatment plant outfall during which dye was observed
bubbling out of the ground during low tide, approximately 110 m from the shore. On
February 22 it was confirmed that the leak was occurring from the 300mm AC (asbestoscement) original outfall line.
The volume and duration of this bypass of the designated treatment works was reported as
unknown. The last outfall inspection prior to the one described in this section that
observed the leak occurred in 2011.

Compliance:

Out

3. MONITORING AND REPORTING REQUIREMENTS, 3.1 Discharge Monitoring, 3.1.1
Sampling and Analysis
3.1.1: The Permittee shall install a suitable sampling facility and obtain a composite sample
