Dear Town of Ladysmith
Re: Non-compliance Advisory Letter, Permit 120, Ladysmith, BC, Effluent
On January 11, 2018, Ministry of Environment and Climate Change Strategy, Environmental Protection Division staff
conducted an inspection of your facility, Town of Ladysmith municipal sewage system located at 245 Oyster Cove Road,
Ladysmith, BC with authorization number 120 under the Environmental Management Act. Ministry staff were
accompanied on site by Curtis Baker, Town of Ladysmith; Geoff Goodall, Town of Ladysmith.
This Advisory, the alleged violations and the circumstances to which it refers will form part of the compliance history of
Town of Ladysmith, and will be taken into account in the event of future non-compliance.
Please note that this authorization is considered to be out of compliance until such a time as it can be confirmed to meet
the authorization requirements.

Inspection Details:
Requirement Description:

1. AUTHORIZED DISCHARGES, 1.1
1.1.1: The discharge of effluent to which this Section is applicable is from a municipal
sewage system as shown on the attached Site Plan A. The reference number (S.E.A.M.
site number) for this discharge is E100004. 1.1.1 The maximum rate at which effluent may
be discharged through the sewage treatment plant is 6 100 m3/d; the annual average rate
of discharge is 3 050 m3/d. Flow in excess of treatment plant capacity is to be discharged
to the bypass outfall. The maximum rate at which effluent may be bypassed is 8 500 m3/d;
the total annual volume which may be discharged to the bypass outfall is 228 000 m3.

Details/Findings:

On January 11, 2018 BC Ministry of Environment and Climate Change Strategy ("ENV")
Environmental Protection Officer Darren Stewart ("Officer Stewart") inspected the Town of
Ladysmith ("ToL") municipal sewage system for compliance with its effluent discharge
permit PE-120. A data review was also performed by Officer Stewart for date range April
2016 thru December 2017 ("Q2 2016 - Q4 2017").
Officer Stewart observed 12 exceedances of the maximum rate of discharge in data
submitted by ToL: October 14, 2016 = 9758 m3/d, October 15, 2016 = 7836 m3/d,
November 02, 2016 = 7808 m3/d, November 26. 2016 = 7301 m3/d, December 20, 2016
= 6103 m3/d, January 18, 2017 = 7472 m3/d, February 15, 2017 = 11332 m3/d, February
16, 2017 = 7511 m3/d, November 14, 2017 = 7427 m3/d, November 15. 2017 = 9125 m3/
d, November 21, 2017 = 6208 m3/d, and November 22, 2017 = 6527 m3/d.
The annual average rates of discharge for 2016 (2725 m3/d) and 2017 (2768 m3/d) were
both compliant with this section.

Details/Findings:

Mr. Goodall informed Officer Stewart that a new treatment plant consisting of a moving bed
bioreactor ("MMBR") was constructed and operational as of June 2016, thus replacing the
Spiragester primary type treatment plant. Officer Stewart observed the MMBR in operation
during the inspection although it is not an authorized work as specified by this section.
Due to changes in Ministry guidance and direction for the Town of Ladysmith regarding an
updated authorization either via a registration under the Municipal Wastewater Regulation
("MWR"), an Operational Certificate under a Liquid Waste management Plan ("LWMP") or
a permit amendment, compliance with this requirement was not determined.

Compliance:

Not Determined

Actions to be taken:

Follow up with the BC Ministry of Environment and Climate Change Strategy, South
Authorizations section which will provide guidance and recommendations for the
appropriate form of authorization for this facility.
Please contact the South Authorizations Section Head, Bryan.Vroom@gov.bc.ca for
further information.

Requirement Description:

3. MONITORING AND REPORTING REQUIREMENTS, 3.1 Discharge Monitoring, 3.1.1
Sampling and Analysis
3.1.1: The Permittee shall install a suitable sampling facility and obtain a composite sample
of the effluent from the treatment plant effluent once every month. The sample is to consist
of four grab samples taken over a two-hour period at maximum flow and mixed to form a
single sample for subsequent analysis. Proper care should be taken in sampling, storing
and transporting the sample to adequately control temperature and avoid contamination,
breakage, etc. Obtain analyses of the sample for the following: 5-day Biochemical Oxygen
Demand; Total Suspended Solids.

Details/Findings:

Officer Stewart observed a suitable sampling facility and composite sampler able to obtain
the necessary samples specified in this section.
A records review showed analyses performed of the samples for 5-day Biochemical
Oxygen Demand and Total Suspended Solids as required, in addition to fecal coliforms.
